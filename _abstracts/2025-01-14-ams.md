We analyze 2011-2022 trends in fine particulate matter (PM 2.5) and its composition in South Korea using a combination of surface and satellite observations interpreted with statistical models and with the GEOS-Chem chemical transport model. We use a synthetic PM 2.5 network, trained on air quality data available from 2011, to investigate PM 2.5 trends prior to 2015 when national monitoring begins. Using a stepwise multi-linear regression (MLR) technique to remove meteorological influence, we find that PM 2.5 has declined by 0.9 µg m-3 a-1 over the study period, with considerable spatial and temporal variability. In the first five years of PM 2.5 monitoring from the surface network (2015-19), meteorologically-normalized annual trends over the Seoul Metropolitan Area (SMA) were flat and winter PM 2.5 concentrations increased.
